# Lead Magnet Master File — Alex Hormozi ($100M Leads)

> A reusable reference for designing lead magnets in any workspace. Distilled
> from the $100M Leads lead-magnet chapter. Use it to design, critique, or
> rebuild any free offer. The Cork Fountain application is at the bottom as a
> worked example — replace it with your own.

---

## TL;DR

A **lead magnet** is a *complete solution to a narrow problem*, valuable enough
that you could charge for it, given away to find who's interested — and, once
solved, it **reveals the next problem that your core offer solves (for money).**

> Salty pretzels at a bar: the pretzels solve hunger (the narrow problem) and
> reveal thirst (the next problem) → they buy a drink (the core offer). The
> drink revenue covers the pretzels and nets a profit.

**Core offer vs. lead magnet:** advertising your core offer goes straight for
the sale (the direct path to money) — **try this first.** Use a lead magnet
when the thing is expensive or buyers need to understand more before they buy.

**The golden rule:** *A person who pays with their time now is more likely to
pay with their money later.* If your lead magnet isn't worth their time, it's
overpriced — even at free. Make it **so good people feel stupid saying no.**

## The 7 steps to an effective lead magnet

### Step 1 — Pick the problem (and who you solve it for)
Use the **problem–solution cycle**: every problem has a solution; every
solution reveals the next problem. Pick a problem that is **narrow + meaningful**,
solve it, and check that **your core offer solves the *next* problem that
appears.** If it does, you have a winner.

> Home-selling example: the broad solution is "sell your home." The narrow
> problems before that — what's it worth, how to raise value, photos, cleaning,
> landscaping, staging, repairs, moving — are all great lead magnets. Solve one
> free; it makes the rest obvious; you've earned trust; you charge to solve the
> remaining ones with your core offer.

**Action:** name the narrow problem you'll solve, then confirm your core offer
solves the next problem it reveals.

### Step 2 — Decide *how* to solve it (the 3 types)
| Type | What it is | Works best when | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Reveal the problem** (diagnosis) | Make them aware of a problem they didn't know they had | The problem **gets worse the longer they wait** | Website speed test ("30% below standard"), posture analysis, termite inspection |
| **2. Sample / trial** | Full but **brief** access to the core offer (limit uses, time, or both) | Core offer is a **recurring** solution to a recurring problem | Costco food samples, a free posture adjustment, a faster-server demo |
| **3. One step of a multi-step process** | Give one valuable step free, sell the rest | Core offer solves a **complex, multi-step** problem | The book itself, one free coat of garage sealant, free finance calculators/templates |

**The dressing-room ideal:** the best lead magnets do **all three at once** —
a fitting room is a *trial* (try it on), *reveals a problem* (you need
something different), and is *step one of a multi-step process* (now you need
pants to match). If you can, design a magnet that trials, diagnoses, and opens
the bigger package simultaneously.

**Action:** pick how you'll solve the narrow problem (one type, or ideally all
three).

### Step 3 — Decide how to *deliver* it (the 4 mechanisms)
| Mechanism | You give them… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Software** | A tool that does the job (spreadsheet, calculator, small app, dashboard) | A stats dashboard that ranks them vs. industry average |
| **Information** | Something to learn (course, guide, interview, tips, pitfalls) | A mini-course on how to write an ad |
| **Services** | Work done for free (audit, adjustment, first layer) | Run their ads free for 30 days |
| **Physical** | Something they hold (chart, sample, book, swag) | A printed book; a sample bottle |

**3 types × 4 mechanisms = up to 12 versions** of a magnet for the *same narrow
problem*. **Make as many as you can and rotate them** — keeps advertising fresh,
low-effort, and shows you which wins (the winner is often surprising).

**Action:** for your magnet, sketch a version in each delivery mechanism, then
pick.

### Step 4 — Test what to **name** it
> Ogilvy: "When you've written your headline, you've spent 80 cents of your
> advertising dollar." ~5× more people read the headline than anything else.

How it's **presented** matters more than anything — improving the
headline/name/display can 2–10× engagement. If no one notices it, no one ever
learns how good it is.

**Test, in this order:** (1) **headline** (most important), (2) **images**,
(3) **sub-headline.**

### Step 5 — Make it easy to consume
Fast, digestible, low-friction. (A 5-minute read, a one-pager, a single tool.)

### Step 6 — Make it **darn good** — *give away the secrets, sell the implementation*
The market judges everything you offer, free or not. You can never give too
much value; you *can* give too little. Goal: **provide more value than the cost
of your core offer, before they buy.** People buy based on how much value they
*expect after* buying — the easiest way to make them expect a lot is to give a
lot first.

> The alternative — sucky fluff — makes prospects think "this person sucks, they
> only give away fluff," and they tell others not to buy. A vicious cycle.

### Step 7 — Make it easy to say **"I want more"** (the CTA)
A good CTA has two parts:
1. **What to do** — clear, simple, direct. *"Call now," "reply yes," "book the
   call."* (Not "don't delay.")
2. **Reasons to act now** — good reasons beat bad reasons, but **any reason
   beats no reason.** Favorites:
   - **Scarcity** (limited supply): the fewer you have, the more it's wanted.
     Best strategy is **reality** — advertise your *real* limits ("I can only
     take 5 per week"). Ethical scarcity from genuine capacity constraints.
   - **Urgency** (limited time): a deadline makes people act faster ("offer
     ends in an hour").
   - **Any reason at all** (the "fraternity party planner"): a reason needn't
     even make sense to lift action. Always give *a* reason.

Two rules for the CTA itself: **don't be clever, be clear**, and **do it
often** — give the clear action + the reason-why repeatedly, not just once.

## The economics: a lead magnet lowers your cost to get a customer

Even if your lead magnet **costs money to deliver**, it should still *lower*
your cost to acquire a customer (CAC). More engaged leads = more chances to
close, and the extra customers more than cover the delivery cost. Spending a
little to deliver a *great* magnet ($25 budget vs. $0) lets you out-value
everyone else, which pulls more strangers into engaged leads and more sales.
Win, win, win.

**Worked example (the numbers that make it click):**

| | Advertise the core offer | Advertise a free lead magnet |
|---|---|---|
| Core-offer profit | $10,000 | $10,000 |
| Ad cost to get a call | $1,000 | $75 (free magnet → ~10× more engage) |
| Magnet delivery cost | $0 | $25 |
| All-in cost per call | $1,000 | **$100** |
| Conversion | close 1 of 3 calls | 1 of 10 magnet recipients buys |
| **Cost to acquire a customer** | **$3,000** | **$1,000** |
| Return on $10k profit | ~3:1 | **~10:1** |

Same ad budget, CAC cut by 3×, so the **business roughly triples.** The goal
isn't your fair share, it's to print money — and giving value *before* the
sale is how experienced operators beat newbies.

## Mental models (keep these handy)

- **Salty pretzels.** The magnet solves a narrow problem *and* reveals the next
  one your core offer sells.
- **Be a (legal) drug dealer.** Give the **strongest hit first** — don't dilute
  the magnet. The free hit gets them hooked; they pay for the next one.
- **Time is a cost.** Free still has a price. Worth-their-time → worth-their-money.
- **Grand slam, even for free.** So good they feel stupid saying no. The
  business that provides the most value wins, period.
- **Make many, rotate.** Up to 12 versions per narrow problem; the winner
  surprises you.
- **Show value before the sale** to make them expect value after the sale.
- **A small delivery budget beats $0.** Spending ~$25 to deliver a genuinely
  better magnet wins more engaged leads than a free-to-make one — it lowers
  CAC, it doesn't raise it.
- **Don't be clever, be clear, and repeat the ask.** The CTA + reason-why
  should appear more than once.

## Design checklist

- [ ] Narrow, meaningful problem chosen
- [ ] Core offer demonstrably solves the *next* problem the magnet reveals
- [ ] Type chosen (reveal / trial / one-step — ideally all three, dressing-room)
- [ ] Delivery mechanism chosen (software / info / service / physical)
- [ ] Name + headline tested (then images, then sub-headline)
- [ ] Easy to consume (fast, digestible, low friction)
- [ ] Genuinely valuable — secrets given, implementation sold; no fluff
- [ ] Strongest hit first (not diluted)
- [ ] CTA = what to do + a reason to act now (scarcity / urgency / any reason), clear not clever, repeated often
- [ ] Worth the prospect's *time* (or it's overpriced at free)
- [ ] Delivery cost (even if >$0) still lowers CAC vs. advertising the core offer directly

## Worked example — Cork Fountain (CI competitive-intelligence product)

- **Narrow problem:** "I don't know what my real Amazon rivals are doing to my
  top SKUs, or what it's costing me." (Solved free.)
- **Next problem revealed → core offer:** it changes every day and I can't watch
  it manually → **continuous monitoring + surfaced moves ($35K/yr).**
- **Type — aim for the dressing room (all three):**
  - *Diagnosis:* map their flagship ASINs to real rivals and surface the top
    moves with **dollar exposure** ("draw the line to dollars at risk").
  - *Trial:* deliver it **inside the live portal** with their real ASINs (a taste
    of the actual recurring product, not a PDF).
  - *One step:* the teardown is step one of the ongoing subscription.
- **Delivery:** primarily **service** (done-for-you teardown) + **software**
  (portal). A scalable **software** sibling — a self-serve "exposure calculator" —
  widens the funnel with zero operator time.
- **Name (test these):** "Competitive Exposure Report," "ASIN Threat Map" — imply
  *quantified loss + insider edge*. (Avoid "Buy Box"-centric names: most brands
  own their Buy Box, so it's one front of several, not the headline.)
- **Make it darn good:** include the actual recommended moves and the dollar
  figure (give the secret); sell the ongoing execution + monitoring.
- **CTA + reason to act now:** "Reply 'yes' and I'll start." + ethical scarcity
  ("I can take on a handful a week") + event urgency (a major Q4 retail window
  approaching — never naming the exact date).
- **Worth their time:** a 5-minute read that quantifies money at stake → signals
  the $35K core offer is worth their money.
